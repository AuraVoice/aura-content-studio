## 1. Remotion: how it works, cloud rendering options, cost, licensing, captions, Vercel limits

### Takeaway
Remotion (React components rendered frame by frame to MP4) is the most flexible code-first option and is now officially supported on Vercel Sandbox via `@remotion/vercel` (experimental), alongside the mature Remotion Lambda path; compute is cents per video. Licensing is free for for-profit companies with up to 3 employees; a 4+ person company needs a Company License, and for unattended server rendering that means "Remotion for Automators" at $0.01 per render with a $100 per month minimum.

### Cited Findings
- Current version: `remotion` 4.0.539, last modified on npm 2026-10-26; `@remotion/captions` 4.0.539 ("Primitives for dealing with captions"); `@remotion/vercel` 4.0.539 ("Render Remotion videos on Vercel Sandbox"). Read directly from the npm registry (`npm view`), [npm: remotion](https://www.npmjs.com/package/remotion)
- License text shipped in the package (copyright 2026): free if you are an individual, a for-profit organization with up to 3 employees, a non-profit, or still evaluating; otherwise "You are required to obtain a Company License". The file also says "In Remotion 5.0, the license will slightly change" with a link to PR #3750. [Remotion LICENSE.md in npm package](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@remotion/bundler@4.0.490/LICENSE.md)
- Official terms (v5.0): renders under "Remotion for Automators" are priced at $0.01 per render, charged in increments of 1,000 renders ($10), with a Minimum Spend of $100 per month; one render = one output video, audio file, still, or image sequence. [Remotion Terms v5.0](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/terms); [Remotion License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)
- Per-render fees apply only if you must license "Remotion for Automators"; Free License or "Remotion for Creators" users do not pay per render. [Remotion License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)
- Conflicting third-party pricing: $25 per seat per month for a Company License (Creators seat) and a combined $100/month minimum; an Enterprise tier from $500/month. One page showing $25/seat was dated 11 Sep 2026. [Wireflow: Remotion pricing 2026](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/remotion-pricing); [autoae: Is Remotion free (2026)](https://autoae.online/blog/is-remotion-free); [Spotsaas review](https://www.spotsaas.com/blog/remotion-review)
- Conflict in the terms about embedding the Remotion Player: one terms URL treats embedding the Player as automation requiring Automators at the minimum spend; another says Free License users may embed it without buying renders. [Remotion Terms](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/terms); [Remotion terms (alt URL)](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/terms)
- Vercel Sandbox path: `@remotion/vercel` is flagged experimental (breaking changes possible), needs `@vercel/sandbox` as a peer dependency. Core API: `createSandbox()` (sandbox with Remotion installed), `addBundleToSandbox()`, `renderMediaOnVercel()`, plus `getRenderProgress()`, `renderStillOnVercel()`, `uploadToVercelBlob()`. Starter: `npx create-video@latest --vercel`. - Remotion Lambda cost: official cost example lists a 1-minute video at $0.021 (2048 MB Lambda, us-east-1, warm) and a 10-minute HD video at $0.162; the Lambda page's benchmark estimates about $0.01 per minute of video (ARM64, 2048 MB, warm), excluding S3 and transfer. Cost depends on region, memory, parallelization and video type, and embedding a video inside a composition "increases the cost significantly". Max session 45 min on Hobby, 24 hours on Pro/Enterprise; vCPUs 1 or even numbers 2 to 32, max 4 (Hobby), 8 (Pro), 32 (Enterprise), 2 GB RAM per vCPU. Concurrency 10 (Hobby), 10,000 (Pro/Enterprise). ### Inferences
- Rough Vercel Sandbox render cost per video (assumption: 8 vCPU / 16 GB Pro sandbox, 5 minutes wall time, CPU fully busy, and that the $0.128/hour rate is per vCPU): CPU 8 x (5/60) x $0.128 = about $0.085; memory 16 x (5/60) x $0.0212 = about $0.028; so roughly $0.11 per render, or about $3 to $4 per month for 30 videos. Render time is the unknown; it must be measured.
- Lambda compute for 15 to 45 minutes per month would be on the order of $1 to $2 even with the "embedded video" penalty, since all shots here are embedded video (avatar + B-roll). The license, not compute, dominates cost if the company has 4+ employees: $100/month minimum.
- Remotion inside a plain Vercel Function is not the path Remotion ships; the official Vercel integration uses Sandbox (a microVM), which sidesteps the Function 250 MB bundle and 800 s duration limits (see section 5). This is consistent with a durable Vercel Workflow step that creates a sandbox, renders detached, polls, then uploads to Blob or Supabase Storage.
- Remotion gives full control of the "edit grammar" (punch-ins as `scale` interpolations, B-roll as `<Sequence>`/`<OffthreadVideo>`, SFX as `<Audio>` at cut frames, music ducking via per-frame `volume` callback) in TypeScript, which matches the stack. This is the option where all creative rules live in versioned code.

- Remotion 5.0 license changes (PR #3750) not reviewed in detail.

## 2. Hosted render APIs: Creatomate, Shotstack, JSON2Video, Plainly, Bannerbear, Editframe, Rendi

### Takeaway
At 15 to 45 minutes per month, every hosted JSON/template render API costs about $15 to $70 per month; Shotstack (per-minute, rich-caption asset with karaoke/highlight word animation, auto transcription via alias) and Creatomate (pixel-based credits, `transcript_source` auto subtitles with word highlight, `webhook_url` callbacks) are the two most directly suited to word-by-word captions without running your own transcription. Rendi is raw FFmpeg-as-a-service billed by GB.

### Cited Findings
- Creatomate credit formula: credits = width x height x fps x seconds / 100,000,000 (minimum 1 per video). 720p25 is about 14 credits/min; 1080p25 is about 31.1 credits/min; at 30 fps about 37 credits/min. Plans: Essential $54/month for 2,000 credits ($45 yearly), Growth $129 for 10,000; Beyond reported as $329 for 50,000 by one source and $249 by another. No ongoing free plan (50 one-time trial credits); credits do not roll over. 1080p25 minute on Essential is about $0.84. [Wireflow: Creatomate pricing 2026](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/creatomate-pricing); [JSON2Video: Creatomate alternative](https://json2video.com/how-to/creatomate-alternative/); [Rendobar: Creatomate alternative](https://rendobar.com/blog/creatomate-alternative/)
- Creatomate auto subtitles: a text element's `transcript_source` points to a video or audio element by id/name; no separate transcription call; a "highlight" effect lights each word as spoken; `transcript_maximum_length` caps characters per segment. Works with voiceover audio elements too. [Creatomate: Add auto-generated subtitles](https://creatomate.com/docs/api/quick-start/add-auto-generated-subtitles); [Creatomate: Auto-generate subtitles in Node.js](https://creatomate.com/how-to/nodejs/auto-generate-subtitles); [Creatomate: JSON-to-video examples](https://creatomate.com/blog/json-to-video-practical-examples)
- Creatomate webhooks: pass `webhook_url` with the POST (or set a project default); fires on succeeded or failed via HTTP POST with the render object (`status`, output `url`, `error_message`, echoed `metadata`). v2 statuses include planned, waiting, transcribing, rendering, succeeded, failed, cancelled (`POST /v2/renders`); v1 docs say renders are stored up to 30 days. Pay-as-you-go reported as $0.30/min (bundles from $75 for 250 credits, valid one year) by some sources and $0.40/min (min 25 credits for $10) by a 2026 review. ## 4. Transcription with word-level timestamps vs TTS alignment

### Takeaway
Word timestamps are cheap everywhere (well under $1/month at this volume); the deciding factors are accuracy and longevity. OpenAI is a poor choice: only `whisper-1` returns word timestamps and it is scheduled for removal on 26 Feb 2027, with no word timestamps announced for `gpt-transcribe`. If the voice is TTS-generated (likely for an avatar), ElevenLabs `with-timestamps` returns character-level alignment for free with the audio, which gives exact script spelling and perfect sync.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI: `timestamp_granularities` (word or segment) requires `response_format=verbose_json`; word timestamps add latency. Third-party tests: `gpt-4o-transcribe` + `verbose_json` returns 400; only `whisper-1` accepts `timestamp_granularities`. `gpt-4o-transcribe-diarize` gives segment start/end, not word timing. [OpenAI API reference: Create transcription](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/audio/subresources/transcriptions/methods/create); [GitHub PR: gate verbose_json to whisper-1](https://github.com/The-Code-Labz/transcript-forge/pull/6)
- OpenAI deprecation: `whisper-1`, `gpt-4o-transcribe`, `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` and `gpt-4o-transcribe-diarize` to be removed from the API on 26 Feb 2027; replacements `gpt-transcribe` ($0.0045/min) and `gpt-live-transcribe` ($0.017/min); prior prices whisper-1 / gpt-4o-transcribe $0.006/min, mini $0.003/min. As of 17 Oct 2026 no word-timestamp support announced for gpt-transcribe; gpt-live-transcribe documented as not returning word-level timestamps. Secondary sources only. ## 5. Raw FFmpeg approach: compositing, ASS karaoke captions, zoom cuts, ducking; where to run it

### Takeaway
Everything required (9:16 compositing, B-roll overlays, punch-in zooms, word-by-word ASS captions burned via libass, sidechain-ducked music, SFX mixing) is achievable with one FFmpeg filtergraph, and open-source examples exist for each piece; the cost is engineering effort and harder iteration on styling. A 60 s render fits easily in Vercel Sandbox (24 h Pro sessions), Cloud Run jobs, Modal or Lambda (15 min), and probably in a Vercel Function (800 s GA) if the ffmpeg binary fits the 250 MB bundle.

### Cited Findings
- Word-highlight ASS captions: tools generate `.ass` and burn in one libass pass; one groups 3 to 5 words and tints/scales the active word with per-word override tags, noting these are more controllable than `\k` karaoke timing. Another writes one Dialogue event per spoken word from Whisper word timestamps then applies the `subtitles` filter. Another offers karaoke, highlight, reveal and pop styles. [GitHub: nik-devs/ffmpeg-caption-burn-ass](https://github.com/nik-devs/ffmpeg-caption-burn-ass); [DEV: word-by-word captions with Python and ffmpeg](https://dev.to/prime619/word-by-word-captions-with-python-and-ffmpeg-no-capcut-watermark-10a); [GitHub: CosLynxAI/ffmpeg-word-captions](https://github.com/CosLynxAI/ffmpeg-word-captions); [n8n workflow: burn karaoke captions with FFmpeg](https://n8n.io/workflows/18486-burn-word-highlighted-karaoke-captions-onto-videos-with-ffmpeg-and-ffprobe)
- Punch-in / Ken Burns zoom on 9:16 built with `scale`, `crop` and `zoompan`, with noted pitfalls. [DEV: AI Shorts pipeline with ffmpeg](https://dev.to/syncoders/building-an-ai-shorts-pipeline-with-ffmpeg-916-ken-burns-zoom-captions-that-match-the-voice-1lc1); Node.js worker example: [DEV: Whisper + FFmpeg in a Node.js worker](https://dev.to/kyle_clipspeedai/auto-caption-generation-whisper-ffmpeg-in-a-nodejs-worker-53b0)
- Ducking: `sidechaincompress` with voice as sidechain, example `threshold=0.02:ratio=4:attack=200:release=1000`, then `amix` voice back in (tested on ffmpeg 6.1). [FFmpeg Cookbook: audio ducking](https://ffmpeg-cookbook.pages.dev/en/articles/audio-ducking/); pitfalls thread: [ffmpeg-user list](https://lists.ffmpeg.org/archives/list/ffmpeg-user@ffmpeg.org/thread/CJH2SEPGGFUCQ3YS4VXB7CKJS5OZAPCN/)

[Vercel Functions limitations](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations); [Vercel changelog: Bun large functions](https://vercel.com/changelog/bun-runtime-now-supports-large-functions-and-extended-max-duration); [Vercel Fluid compute](https://vercel.com/docs/fluid-compute)
- Vercel Sandbox: Pro sessions up to 24 h, up to 8 vCPU / 16 GB (32 vCPU Enterprise), $0.128/hr active CPU, $0.0212/GB-hr memory. [Vercel Sandbox pricing and quotas](https://vercel.com/docs/sandbox/pricing)

Conflicting figures exist on other aggregators. [Beam: Modal pricing explained](https://www.beam.cloud/blog/modal-pricing-explained); [Morph: Modal pricing](https://www.morphllm.com/modal-pricing)

### Inferences
- Example filtergraph flow for one video (illustrative): avatar.mp4 -> `scale/crop` to 1080x1920 -> `zoompan` or `scale+crop` keyed by an expression on `t` for punch-ins at cut points -> `overlay` B-roll clips with `enable='between(t,a,b)'` -> `subtitles=captions.ass` (word-by-word) -> `drawtext` or a second ASS layer for on-screen hook text; audio: voice + `sidechaincompress` music + `adelay`'d SFX hits -> `amix` -> `loudnorm` -> H.264/AAC MP4 with `+faststart`.
- All timings (cuts, zooms, SFX hits, caption words) come from one JSON "edit decision list" produced by a specialist agent, and a TypeScript function compiles it to ASS + filtergraph. This keeps the LangGraph state structured and the renderer deterministic.
- FFmpeg cost at this volume is negligible on any host; Vercel Sandbox keeps everything on one vendor and avoids the 250 MB bundle and 800 s caps, and pairs naturally with a durable Workflow step.
- Trade-off vs Remotion: FFmpeg is cheaper to license (free, LGPL/GPL builds) and faster to render, but animated captions beyond what ASS override tags can do (spring/bounce pops, emoji, per-word background boxes with easing) are much harder than in React.

- AWS Lambda limits not confirmed from an AWS primary page in this session.
- FFmpeg licensing implications (GPL builds with libx264 for a commercial service) not researched.

## 7. Retention edit grammar, caption styles and safe zones (1080x1920)

### Takeaway
Tools and guides encode roughly: a visual change every 2 to 4 seconds (cut, 10 to 15% punch-in, B-roll, or text), word-by-word captions in a heavy all-caps sans with the active word colored, and keeping text out of the platform UI bands. A single layout that satisfies Reels, TikTok and Shorts at 1080x1920 is roughly x 120 to 888 and y 288 to 1248 (768 x 960 px), built from the most restrictive published margins.

### Cited Findings

Word-by-word text appearing counts as a change. 85% of social video watched without sound (secondary claim).
